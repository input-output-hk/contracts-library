## Step by step

A few conventions make the cards easier to read:

- **Addresses are roles**, not real addresses: `vesting_addr`,
  `beneficiary_addr`, `locker_addr`.
- **Values name tokens by role**: `ADA`.
- **The datum is immutable.** The lock shows the terms; a continuation leaves
  every field blank because it must reproduce the datum byte-for-byte.
- **Validity ranges are symbolic**: `now`, `recovery + 1`.
- **Fee and collateral UTxOs are not drawn.** The validator asserts nothing
  about them — only about its own input, the continuing outputs, the validity
  range and the authorization.

`beneficiary` claims the vested portion; `locker` locks the bundle and cancels
the remainder. Both are pluggable credentials — see <a href={docUrl("vesting/spec.md")}>spec §2</a>. Keys are shown in the cards; a script beneficiary or locker produces the same shape with a `withdrawals` entry.

## Lock

Creates the vesting UTxO. No script runs; the builder is responsible for the

## Claim

Withdraws the vested portion. A partial claim leaves a continuation with the
same datum; a full claim at or after `end_time` leaves none.

## Cancel

After `recovery_time`, the locker recovers whatever remains. The timing check is
strict: `now > recovery_time`.

## Batching and identical instances

The validator accepts any number of vesting UTxOs in one transaction. For inputs
that share a byte-identical datum it requires at least `k` continuations, each
holding the full remainder, where `k` is the number of such inputs. That closes
both double satisfaction and the merge attack: one continuation cannot cover
several inputs, and several instances cannot be collapsed into fewer UTxOs.

The MeshJS reference builder currently builds one instance per transaction.
Compose additional inputs yourself if you need to batch.
