The [overview](/contracts/event-triggered-assets) shows the certificate
lifecycles and the one-time registration. Here is every action, drawn. The <a href={docUrl("event-triggered-assets/spec.md")}>specification §4</a> owns the exact constraints.

## Step by step

A few conventions make the cards easier to read:

- **Addresses are roles**: `issuer_addr`, `registry_addr`, `always_fail_addr`
  (the issuance template), `vault_addr`, `plb_addr [stake: …]`,
  `depositor_addr`, `holder_addr`, `holder_payment_addr`, `pay_addr`,
  `protocol_params`.
- **Token names carry meaning**: `registryNode`, `issuanceTemplate`,
  `cipNFT (step k)` is the certificate at step `k`, `coupon (amount)` is a
  coupon worth that many lovelace, and `vaultNFT` authenticates a vault.
- **The certificate datum is** `start`, `commit`, `cnt_policy`; the vault datum
  is its `cnt_policy`.
- **Values are symbolic**: `principal`, `funds`, `amount_k`, `min_ada`, `f`.
- **Fee and collateral UTxOs are not drawn.**

`holder` signs claims and transfers; `issuer` registers the instrument and, for
committed certificates, triggers claims and graduation against the committed
credential. See <a href={docUrl("event-triggered-assets/spec.md")}>spec §2</a>.

## Registry and issuance

### Register

The issuer inserts the RegistryNode once. No certificate is minted here; the
node is a reference input for every later deposit.

<TxDiagram data={register} />

### Deposit (unit)

Anyone deposits one unit: the principal moves into the vault and the certificate
is minted to the depositor at step 0.

<TxDiagram data={deposit} />

## Certificate custody

### Transfer

The certificate moves freely; the step, `start` and `cnt_policy` are preserved.

<TxDiagram data={transfer} />

### Authorize (self-transfer)

The owner commits a payment credential for issuer triggering, or clears it to
revoke. The certificate stays with the same owner.

<TxDiagram data={authorize} />

## Coupons

### Coupon claim (step k)

The holder burns the certificate's name `k-1`, mints the name `k`, and receives
the step's coupon.

<TxDiagram data={couponClaim} />

### Issuer claim (step k)

The issuer triggers the same step on the owner's behalf, paying the committed
credential. The paired output is the next certificate, so no ghost shell remains.

<TxDiagram data={issuerClaim} />

### Redemption (burn-to-pay)

The coupon is burned at the vault; the vault pays exactly the value encoded in
the burned coupon names and keeps the rest.

<TxDiagram data={redemption} />

## Maturity

### Graduation

At maturity the holder claims the principal-only coupon and retires the
certificate.

<TxDiagram data={graduation} />

### Issuer graduation

The issuer retires the certificate on the owner's behalf and pays the principal
coupon to the commitment. The paired PLB output is a certificate-less ghost
shell.

<TxDiagram data={issuerGraduation} />
