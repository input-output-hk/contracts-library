**Event-Triggered Assets** is a tokenized bond backed by ADA vaults. The issuer
registers the instrument once; anyone can then deposit a fixed bond unit into a
vault and receive a CIP-113 **certificate** for it. The certificate records the
unit's timeline and its vault's coupon policy. Its asset name is the current
**step**, and each claim advances it in place.

Each scheduled step mints a **coupon token** whose asset name is its ADA value.
A coupon is redeemed by burning it at the vault, which pays exactly what the
burned coupons name. At maturity the certificate is retired and a principal
coupon is minted. **Issuer triggering is opt-in**: the owner commits a payment
credential with a self-transfer, after which the issuer can trigger claims and
graduation on the owner's behalf, always paying that credential.

## Issuer-triggered lifecycle

The issuer registers the instrument once with `Register` (on the
[transactions page](/contracts/event-triggered-assets/transactions)); every
deposit references that RegistryNode. After a deposit, the owner can commit a
payment credential with `Authorize`. The issuer then triggers every coupon claim
and graduation, always paying the commitment. The owner can revoke at any time
and always keeps the holder path.

## Holder lifecycle

The holder signs each claim directly. After a coupon claim, the coupon can be
redeemed at the vault (top branch) or the certificate can continue to the next
step (bottom branch). The redemption branch is terminal; the certificate path
ends at graduation.

## When to use it

- Tokenized bonds or fixed-income instruments with a coupon schedule backed by
  ADA.
- Claims that should be transferable and independently timed per deposit.
- Instruments where the issuer should be able to service holders passively —
  after an explicit, revocable commitment.
- Setups with several vaults, each with its own coupon policy.

**When not to:** if you need a fungible principal token in the float, if you
cannot trust the vault owner to keep the vault funded, or if the coupon schedule
must be more expressive than a fixed interval and amount per step.

## What this does not protect

The full list is in <a href={docUrl("event-triggered-assets/spec.md")}>§7 of the specification</a>. The three that change how you deploy:

- **Vault solvency is the central assumption.** Vaults have no owner
  withdrawal path, but the owner funds them off-chain: an underfunded vault
  makes redemptions fail until it is topped up.
- **Issuance is uncapped and redemption is first-come-first-served.** Deposits
  are permissionless; the owner must manage total issuance off-chain and fund
  every certificate's coupons.
- **The CIP-113 core is trusted infrastructure.** The PLB, transfer and
  issuance validators custody and move the certificate.
